# Worked Example — Industrial Compressor Positioning (Synthetic)

> This is a fabricated teaching example. No real company, customer interview, energy audit, market share, or performance outcome is represented.

## Starting point
A fictional equipment supplier competes in industrial compressed-air systems. Its current message says “reliable quality, advanced technology, and professional service.” These claims do not establish a meaningful difference because competitors can make similar claims.

## Research hypothesis
Some factories with long operating hours may care more about total lifecycle energy cost and uptime than initial purchase price alone. This is a hypothesis, not a market fact.

## Candidate positioning
For mid-sized factories with high-utilization production lines, Northstar Air Systems is an energy-efficient compressed-air solution provider that helps manage lifecycle energy cost through equipment selection, commissioning, leak audits, and ongoing optimization.

## Why it might work
- It reframes comparison from purchase price to lifecycle cost.
- It ties equipment to a service system.
- It could create recurring customer contact and measurable outcomes.

## Why it might fail
- Buyers may be evaluated on capital expenditure, not total cost of ownership.
- Energy consumption may be dominated by site conditions and operations.
- Competitors may offer the same audit or service package.
- The supplier may not have enough service density to deliver profitably.

## Required validation
1. Interview buyers, operators, and maintenance leaders separately.
2. Collect real utility bills, compressor run hours, load profiles, and maintenance records with permission.
3. Compare products under equivalent duty cycles and measurement conditions.
4. Test willingness to pay for lifecycle support.
5. Run a limited pilot with a pre-defined baseline and comparison.
6. Track energy per unit of production, uptime, service cost, retention, and contribution margin.

## Decision
Do not scale based on the positioning statement alone. Proceed only if customer relevance, proof, service delivery, and economics pass their gates.
